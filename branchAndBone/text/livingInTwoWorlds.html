Living in Two Worlds

From the day of that Kansas bike ride to the present day, I have lived in two worlds. Primarily, and by default, it is a version of the human world I was born into.

By aspiration, by cultivation, I sometimes live, at least partly, in the forest world. From the crucial late 20th century symbolic anthropologist Clifford Geertz:

Believing, with Max Weber, that man is an animal suspended in webs of significance he himself has spun, I take culture to be those webs, and the analysis of it to be therefore not an experimental science in search of law but an interpretive one in search of meaning.
The Welsh Marxist literary scholar Raymond Williams coined the term "structure of feeling" to describe the actively lived, shared affective experience of a period or generation before it hardens into formal ideology or fixed institutions. It captures "thought as felt and feeling as thought" — the subtle patterns of impulse, tone, and practical consciousness that people live through in the present.

Thus an ideology in the making, before it takes a full stable form. This is what was born in me that day in the forest. Giving it form has been a lifelong project.
