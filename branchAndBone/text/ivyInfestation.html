Aggressive English Ivy Takes Over

Seward's forest in approximately its current form has been on the peninsula for about 6000 years. The fire return interval (FRI) - for stand replacement fires - is about 500 years. So this forest probably burned to the ground twelve times. But with no permanent effect: even such catastrophic events as these are routinely recovered from.

But even this reslient forest can meet its match.

English Ivy, introduced to Washington state in 1889, is quite another story. It is a cunning interloper for which the forest community has no defense, and no response. Ivy flourishes on the ground, on tree trunks, and in tree canopies. It grows year round. It is toxic. It is hugely destructive.

If left to grow unchecked English Ivy will destroy the forest. Given enough time - perhaps 10,000 years? - other species would arrive or evolve; a new balance might be struck, perhaps the forest would return.

So a sustained community effort was organized to remove large amounts of ivy in 2004. Then Seattle Parks contractors and a few of us volunteers, just a few years ago, cleared the area in this photo. We check back regularly to see if English Ivy, Himalayan Blackberry or English Holly has found a foothold.
