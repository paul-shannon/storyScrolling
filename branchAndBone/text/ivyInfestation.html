Aggressive English Ivy Takes Over

Seward's forest in approximately its current form has been on the peninsula for about 6000 years. The fire return interval (FRI) - for stand replacement fires - is about 500 years. So perhaps this forest burned to the ground a dozen times over its most recent post-glacial incarnation. But with no permanent effect: even such catastrophic events as these are routinely recovered from.

But even this reslient forest can meet its match.

English Ivy, introduced to Washington state in 1889, is quite another story. It is a cunning interloper for which the forest community has no defense, and no response. Ivy flourishes on the ground, on tree trunks, and in tree canopies. It grows year round. It is toxic. It is hugely destructive.

If left to grow unchecked English Ivy will destroy the forest. Given enough time - perhaps 10,000 years? - other species might arrive or evolve; a new balance might be struck, perhaps the forest would return.

A big community effort removed large amounts of ivy in 2004. Then just a few years ago, Seattle Parks contractors and a few of us volunteers cleared the site show in this photo. We check back regularly, here and throughout the forest, for English Ivy, Himalayan Blackberry or English Holly, root them out where we find them, correct for the unintended consequences of our actions.
