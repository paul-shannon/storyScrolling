I encountered my first forest at age seven while riding my bike on the military base where I then lived. I came upon a sun-dappled stream running beneath a low bridge. There were no other people around, only tall trees, understory and the stream. Of course I had seen trees before, and surely a river or two. But I had never seen such an entire interwoven green world (as it then seemed to me) — growing on its own terms with such beauty. A place where I felt at home.

That early encounter posed a question I have been studying on ever since.

What is the relationship between this green world, of sunlight and water, of leaf and limb, and our customary human world, of straight lines, right angles, and things built and extracted?
I was an awkward boy in a complicated family who led the itinerant military life, few social connections, no extended family. This place, and the contrast it offered, kicked off my lifelong pursuit of that question. I figured that this green world, ancient and usually resilient, beauty untouched by human hands or thought, might have some lessons to teach.

This website offers a place for me to work out some of that.
