I encountered my first forest at age seven while riding my bike on the military base where I then lived. I came upon a sun-dappled stream running beneath a low bridge. There were no other people around, only tall trees, understory and the stream. I had of course seen trees before, and surely a river or two. But never an entire interwoven green world (as it then seemed to me) — growing on its own terms with such beauty. A place where I felt at home.

That early encounter posed a question I have been working on ever since. What is the relationship between this green world, of sunlight and water, of leaf and limb, and our customary human world, where things of value are mostly people and things we have built or can extract? As an awkward boy in a complicated family leading the itinerant military life, this place kicked off a lifelong pursuit of that question. The green world, ancient and usually resilient, might have some lessons to teach. This website offers a place for me to work out some of that.
