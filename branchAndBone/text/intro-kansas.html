I discovered my first forest at age seven on a bike ride on the military base where I then lived. Riding without any particular destination, I came upon a sun-dappled stream running beneath a low bridge. There were no other people around, only tall trees, the understory and the stream.

That was my first encounter with a world born not made, a place grown not built. I was a small awkward boy with a severe stutter.

We humans often theorize our world with the biggest most compelling and powerful objects around us. Planted into my imagination that day was the possibility that these these tall trees, the dappled stream, the blue sky and bright sun, with no people in sight, that these big things made up a coherent world into which I was welcome, even as I did not know much about it.

My life at that time was complicated and fraught: speech was such a struggle, my parents were emotionally distant. My father was an army officer building nuclear-capable tactical missiles. My mother was a once glamorous woman locked into the role of the Army Wife. I was the kid with the speech impediment.

There alone on that day, in that forest, the complexity and my awkwardness slipped away. I felt at home in a beautiful world.
