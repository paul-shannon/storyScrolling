There have been many forests for me since then — in the Blue Ridge Mountains of Virginia, and on old dirt roads through the mountains of West Virgina. And for the last thirty years, a small forest in Seattle, on a peninsula, in a city park. It is old-growth, a full 500 years old, a rare remnant of the million acres of forest once found here in the Puget Lowlands, between the Cascade Mountains to the east, and the Olympic Mountains to the west. Giant Douglas-firs, tiny Douglas squirrels. Pileated woodpecker and crowned kinglets. Hemlock and Cedar. Eagle and owl.

This old forest is where I go — somtimes , as when I was a kid, on my bike. I do my best to take in its complex beauty, maybe to learn some of the secrets of the green world.

Some of that elegance, some of those secrets, are found below.
