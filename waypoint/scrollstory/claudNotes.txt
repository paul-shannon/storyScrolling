--- note 2, showing how to add a <video> link

Your `<video>` is now supported, and a scene can hold it, the YouTube embed, or both. I rebuilt the page and the output contains the `<video>` tag, its `<source>`, and the YouTube iframe. As before, I haven't played it in a browser. The changes are in `story.yaml`, `template.html.j2`, `build.py`, `story.css` and `story.js`.

In `story.yaml`, the Big Trees scene now lists both items, stacked in the pinned panel:

```yaml
    media:
      - video: https://sewardpark.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/bigTree-7.mov
        controls: true
        muted: true
        alt: "Video of the mature Douglas-fir"
      - youtube: NRlrcbBItVQ
        start: 955
        end: 957
```

Drop either entry to show just one. A remote video link needs no CORS setup, because the browser's `<video>` element can play a file from another site. The options are:
- **`controls: true`** gives a player the reader controls. Without it, a video is a muted looping background that plays only while its scene is on screen.
- **`muted: true`** matches your original snippet. The reader can unmute.
- **`poster:`** is an optional still image shown before play. I'd add one, since iPhones often show a blank frame without it.

Two differences from your HTML, both deliberate:
- **`preload="metadata"`** replaces your bare `preload`. A bare `preload` means "download it all", which would pull a large `.mov` over a phone connection as soon as the page loaded. Metadata mode fetches only the start.
- **Reader-controlled videos never autoplay.** They also pause when scrolled out of view.

I left the `<source>` without a `type` attribute, as in your snippet. A `type="video/quicktime"` attribute can make Chrome skip the file.

**The format is the main risk.** A `.mov` from an iPhone is often HEVC, which won't play in Chrome or Firefox, and a regular H.264 `.mov` plays in most browsers but isn't guaranteed everywhere. Check it with:

```bash
ffprobe -v error -show_entries stream=codec_name -of csv=p=0 bigTree-7.mov
```

If you see `hevc`, or want a smaller file and fast start, make a web copy and a poster frame:

```bash
ffmpeg -i bigTree-7.mov -c:v libx264 -crf 23 -preset slow -c:a aac -movflags +faststart bigTree-7.mp4
ffmpeg -ss 5 -i bigTree-7.mov -frames:v 1 bigTree-7-poster.jpg
```

Then point `video:` at the `.mp4` and add `poster:`. Hosting the video
on sewardpark.org keeps it independent of your story site, but the
page depends on that file staying at that address.



--- first demo (10 nov 2026)

I've built the starter as a `scrollstory/` folder, and it builds, but I haven't opened the page in a browser, so the look and the scrolling are untested. Put the folder inside your `waypoint/` directory, next to `florilegium/`, so the relative paths work. The build output confirms five scenes, and the JavaScript passes a syntax check.

```bash
pip install jinja2 markdown pyyaml
cd waypoint/scrollstory
make all        # link media, build index.html, serve at http://localhost:4441
```

Keep `make` in that folder and open it over `http://localhost`, not `file://`, so the YouTube video loads.

The five scenes use your florilegium passages:
1. **Title** (`full` layout): a gradient with the heading.
2. **Snyder, "Front Lines"** (`overlay`): the poem sits on a card over a pinned full-bleed image.
3. **Rexroth** (`reading`): a plain paper column with no media, as the poem and the essay passages want.
4. **Nick Lane on mitochondria** (`overlay`): your `mitochondria.webp` is pinned behind the card.
5. **Big Trees, Burned Bark** (`split`): your side-by-side layout with the YouTube video (`start: 955`, `end: 957`, the 2-second range from your commented-out line) and the passage.

**What's missing.** Your repo has one image, `mitochondria.webp`, and no local video. The Snyder scene points to `media/night-forest.jpg`, which doesn't exist. The build reports it and the scene falls back to a dark gradient. Drop any photo or `.mp4` into `media/` (the makefile links it to `../florilegium/img`) and set the `img:` or `video:` field in `story.yaml`. For a video, add `poster:` as well. Videos play muted and only while their scene is on screen, and they stay still if the visitor prefers reduced motion.

**How it works**
- **Text:** Passages come from your existing HTML fragments, from new `.md` files, or from the Big Trees text I extracted out of your old YAML.
- **Active card:** The card nearest the middle of the screen brightens and the others fade. Without JavaScript, everything stays fully readable.
- **Progress bar:** The thin bar at the top follows the page scroll using CSS only. Firefox doesn't show it yet, which is harmless.
- **Layouts:** Each scene's `layout:` field picks `full`, `overlay`, `reading` or `split`. Adding a new one means one block in `template.html.j2` and some rules in `story.css`.

Things worth checking when you first open it: whether the cards have
enough contrast on your real photos, how long the 55vh gaps between
cards feel, and whether the sticky image behaves on your phone and
iPad. If any of these look wrong, tell me what you see and I'll
adjust.
